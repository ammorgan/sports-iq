# RepQuest Stakeholder Gap Assessment

**Assessment date:** August 16, 2026

**Stage:** Product beta

## Executive view

RepQuest has a differentiated, worthwhile concept: beginner-first sports literacy across many sports, with age-adaptive learning and role-specific paths. The brand, avatars, sport catalog, progression, and game-like direction are strong enough to support further investment. It is not production-ready yet. The largest gap is not visual polish; it is the distance between a convincing prototype and a safe, repeatable learning platform with reviewed curriculum, real accounts, consent, verified media rights, and measurable learning outcomes.

## What is strong

- Clear mission with both youth and adult value.
- Broad sports catalog and a reusable general-to-position curriculum structure.
- Age-specific timers and a no-timer 7–9 path.
- Positive treatment of incorrect answers.
- Memorable RepQuest name, energetic arena visual language, diverse avatars, and XP progression.
- Shared curriculum direction across web and mobile.
- Conservative account gate that refuses to collect family data before secure services are ready.

## Priority gaps

### 1. Curriculum depth and instructional sequencing — critical

The platform now gives all 152 positions/events an age-aware foundation, tactical, and mastery path, and every advanced quiz is preceded by a lesson built from the assessed decisions. Position IQ remains immediately selectable and later levels require the prior level only within that role. The remaining critical gap is editorial validation: generated modules need named sport-SME approval, diagrams where spatial understanding matters, reading-level validation, and evidence that each check was explicitly taught.

### 2. Early-reader experience — critical

Shorter text alone is insufficient for ages 7–9. The product needs illustrated choices, optional narration, one-action screens, visual progress, fewer question types, and testing with real early readers. Reading level should be measured, not estimated informally.

### 3. Authentication, consent, and cloud progress — critical

The account UI is a safe placeholder, not working authentication. There is no complete session, sync, row-level authorization, parental-consent system, recovery, or deletion lifecycle. These must be implemented and security-tested before online child accounts or public leaderboards.

### 4. Legal/privacy operations — critical

Draft Terms and Privacy pages now exist, but they require the operator’s legal identity, contact information, final vendor/data inventory, retention policy, parental notice, consent method, and counsel review. App Store/Play disclosures and account deletion must match actual behavior.

### 5. Content credibility and media rights — high

All 24 sports now have registered governing-source anchors and every generated module declares its source IDs. This is traceability, not sport-SME approval. Wrestling remains unmapped, MMA is explicitly labeled as a striking example rather than a complete overview, water polo and gymnastics remain hidden pending review, and matching flag-football and lacrosse excerpts are quarantined outside distributable assets. The remaining library is held to the same standard: exact sport match, useful six-to-eight-second teaching moment, provenance, publication date, review status, and re-review date.

### 6. Information density and next-action clarity — high

The main home direction is intuitive, but several sport screens contain too much explanation before action. The first Start/Continue action should remain above optional video and path detail. Reduce duplicate descriptions, avoid internal labels such as “General Sport IQ” without a plain-language cue, and keep one primary action per screen.

### 7. Progress integrity and progression design — high

The beta now uses one age-aware eligible catalog for web/mobile percentages, sequential prerequisites within each general or specialized path, explicit legacy-ID migration, and first-completion-only XP. XP and progress are still client-controlled, which is acceptable only for private guest play. Cloud progress still needs idempotent server-owned events, replay limits, fraud controls, and explicit mastery criteria before public leaderboards.

### 8. Accessibility and inclusive design — high

The experience needs systematic screen-reader, keyboard, dynamic-type, contrast, reduced-motion, captions/transcripts, and cognitive-load testing. Audio cannot become required for early readers; narration must complement equivalent visual/text content.

### 9. Product measurement — medium

There is no validated learning metric or privacy-safe funnel instrumentation yet. Track lesson starts/completions, concept recovery, abandonment, media failures, and post-lesson knowledge without exact-age profiling or unnecessary child tracking.

### 10. Operational readiness — medium

Before launch, RepQuest needs content administration, review workflows, monitoring, support processes, moderation for any public feature, incident response, backups/restore tests, and release checklists.

## Add

- Illustrated “What is this sport?” and “Meet the positions/events” lessons.
- Optional read-aloud narration and replayable visual demonstrations.
- A content source/rights/review registry.
- Managed authentication, adult-owned child profiles, consent, secure sync, and deletion.
- Private youth milestones and carefully moderated 13+ opt-in leaderboard.
- Privacy choices/account deletion pages and in-app controls.
- Usability tests with 7–9, 10–12, teen, adult beginner, and parent cohorts.
- Content flags such as “Was this confusing?” tied to a question ID.

## Remove or defer

- Any generic or incorrect sport clip.
- Public youth rankings, messaging, followers, and user-generated content.
- Repeated marketing copy once a user is inside a learning path.
- Advanced terms before definitions.
- Client-authoritative XP in any public competition.
- Large catalog expansion until each current sport meets the completeness rubric.

## Intuitiveness verdict

The home selection and brand direction are intuitive. General IQ and Position/Event IQ are now clearly parallel choices, and real role roadmaps replace placeholder future cards. The complete journey still needs observed usability testing with true beginners and early readers. The next usability milestone is visual instruction and comprehension evidence—not more text or more sports.

## Investment recommendation

Continue the beta with a release-gate quality phase. The shared depth architecture now covers all sports; validate it by sampling every sport and requiring deeper SME sign-off for high-variance disciplines and roles. In parallel, finish legal/operator decisions and secure account architecture. A stakeholder should fund curriculum review, diagrams, youth UX research, media acquisition, and privacy/security infrastructure ahead of additional cosmetic features.
